This week featured another major pivot in my therapeutic journey. Earth-shattering in a sense. I'm really getting to feel that sense of [groundlessness](https://dharmaseed.org/talks/82822/) I've been reading about in the Buddhist texts I've been studying.   

A few months ago, a song lyric from the song ["Your Ex-Lover is Dead"](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=r5Or6-HOveg) became very meaningful to me. It's a spoken line at the beginning of the song that says "When there's nothing left to burn you have to set yourself on fire." I take that line to mean that once you've stopped looking externally for causes and solutions to your problems, there's only one place else to look: at yourself. And I thought I had indeed set myself on fire, but this week it became clear to me that I was still looking externally, at least some of the time. No longer.  

I'm keeping things vague intentionally, but due to a combination of my anxious-attachment style and ADHD (and potentially many other reasons), I became over-invested in making a new friendship closer on an accelerated timeline rather than letting it happen organically. What I've been calling producing not participating. And, until last week, I thought I had actually stopped producing and *was* participating, until until someone didn't respond to a text I'd sent, triggering a particularly dark Sunday night.

I brought that to therapy and we basically agreed that an intervention was in order. I'm only capable of two speeds right now: stopped and pedal to the metal. I lack a throttle so, to develop one, I've got a plan in place. A way to break out of a bad pattern. A way to force me to sit with uncertainty and groundlessness without acting to resolve or ease it. It's hard. One of the hardest things I've ever done, in fact, but it's working. Even a few days in, I can feel that.
